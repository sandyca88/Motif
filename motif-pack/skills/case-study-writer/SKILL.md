# Case Study Writer

Hiring managers skim for three things: **your role, your thinking, and the outcome.** Everything in the case study serves those.

## 1. Interview the notes

Extract, or ask for, only what's missing (at most 5 questions, asked together):

- Project, company/client, timeline, team and **your specific role**
- The problem: who was affected and how we knew (data, research, complaints)
- Constraints: technical, time, business, legal
- 2–3 key decisions and the alternatives you rejected
- Outcome: metrics, qualitative results, what shipped
- What you'd do differently

Mark anything confidential; replace with relative numbers ("+32%") or anonymized names.

## 2. Structure (default)

1. **Headline**: outcome-first, ≤ 12 words: "Redesigning onboarding to lift activation 32% at Brightpay".
2. **Snapshot row**: Role · Team · Timeline · Tools · Platform.
3. **TL;DR (the 30-second version)**: 3 bullets: problem, what I did, result.
4. **The problem**: context + evidence, 80–120 words, one supporting artifact (chart, quote, funnel).
5. **Goals & constraints**: bullet list with success metrics.
6. **Process highlights**: not every step, only the 2–4 moments that changed the direction. Each: what we learned → what we decided.
7. **Key decisions**: for each, "We chose X over Y because Z", with a before/after or option comparison.
8. **The solution**: 3–5 annotated screens; captions explain *why*, not *what*.
9. **Impact**: big numbers + one stakeholder or user quote.
10. **Reflection**: what I'd do next / learned (2–3 sentences, honest).

## 3. Writing rules

- First person singular for your contributions, "we" for team work. Be precise about ownership.
- Active voice, plain language, no buzzwords ("leveraged synergies").
- Every section ≤ 150 words. Total 800–1,200 words for a full case study.
- Captions: "Moving plan selection before sign-up cut drop-off at step 2 by half" beats "Plan selection screen".
- Include one moment of tension or a wrong turn: it shows judgment.

## 4. Deliverables

1. The case study in Markdown.
2. A **30-second version** (≤ 90 words) for portfolio cards and LinkedIn.
3. A **5-slide interview version** outline (problem, insight, decision, solution, impact).
4. Optional: a responsive `case-study.html` page (editorial layout, big type, image placeholders with captions, metadata row, sticky section nav), and an image shot list of which artifacts to export from Figma.

## Quality check

- Can someone state your role and the result after reading only the headline and TL;DR?
- Is every image paired with a "why" caption?
- Are claims backed by a number, quote or artifact?
